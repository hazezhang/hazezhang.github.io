01 / Point of view

Research is most alive when it becomes something people can see, question, and use.

I began in data science by being good at learning. I found my own direction when I started building systems around questions I genuinely cared about.

Today, I’m drawn to the space between a model and the person relying on it: how human judgments become data, how evaluation shapes behavior, and how research survives contact with a real interface, workflow, or decision.

03 / Research direction

Human judgment is not noise to remove. It is part of the problem.

My current research centers on how AI can evaluate complex human performance without collapsing it into a single opaque score.

01

Rubric-aligned evaluation

Can model outputs map to criteria practitioners already understand and use?

02

Quality × timing

How should perceived latency combine what was said with when it arrived?

03

Diagnostic feedback

Can evaluation move beyond ranking toward feedback that helps interpreters improve?

04

Reliable supervision

How can student annotations and limited professional ratings work together responsibly?
